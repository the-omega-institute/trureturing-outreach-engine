# Academic email voice

- Open a reply with a brief thanks and go straight to what we did because of their message. Let the action show how we took up their suggestion.
- Write one connected letter. Let each paragraph pick up a named result, question or idea from the previous one.
- Give each sentence one concrete job and name what it refers to, as in “Mathesis, a preprint and AI review platform.”
- When the project belongs in the exchange, show the loop. A researcher brings a question or direction, the system searches, computes exactly and checks formally, and the researcher's next comment steers the next round.
- Describe the project through the loop and the people in it; counts of solved problems are left out. Say what the loop produces. Use verified counts of collaborating researchers and countries to describe its reach, and show how it helped a specific person on a concrete question.
- Open a collaboration with enough context to show what we can do together. Say we would like to collaborate and ask what they would like to try.
- For an open decision, ask for the facts the owner needs to decide. Use affirmative wording for choices she has already made.
- Use plain, modest English and contractions. Join thoughts with commas, periods or “and.” Use “I” for personal matters and “we” for shared work.
- Use the approved first-contact opening and closing and the exact Mathesis submission sentence where the workflow calls for them.

## Example

Dear Professor A,

Thank you for the simpler formulation. We checked it against the definitions and found that the two versions agree for connected graphs. The attached note gives the argument and a three-vertex example showing why the condition matters.

Best regards,
[Configured sign-off]
