# Review the scientific deliverable

Read the exact original claim, latest source version, complete focused proof,
references and actual PDF/source/evidence artifacts. Distinguish a written proof,
a finite computation and a Lean-checked proposition. Verify that definitions
match the source and that any value/novelty claim is supported by literature.
BOUNCE a note whose title or abstract presents a previously proved result as
new, that restates a passed sibling note's results without citing it, or that
labels a counterexample 'refuted-and-corrected' without a corrected statement.
Use the research-paper-writing-rigor instructions for note quality. Every core
claim needs a complete argument. Reuse existing results with attribution.

For a short note, check source/PDF consistency, all citations, readable layout,
rebuild instructions and all problem IDs it covers. A merged note needs an
explicit coverage mapping. The presence of a PDF is not scientific approval.
Check the final result's `formalization_gaps` against all statements in the
PDF and the precise scope of pinned Lean declarations. Verify each gap's
statement, both locators, public source and significance. An empty list means
the coverage check found no gap. Check the `value_assessment` against the proof
and the closest literature. A trivial note cannot PASS. The coordinator owns
additional value uplift and formalization filing.
For prior-work filing, match the posed statement and locator to the exact
public upstream result. Verify each citation's public URL, release date,
exact locator and what it settles.
Check the trureturing resolution date against its upstream record when cited.
A talk's title, venue and date require a public talk page or slides. Keep
private reports out of the structured `prior_work_check` and public issue text.
Check the structured public citations against the evidence section when both exist.
For short notes, independently review the structured `author_contacts` in the
exact result: primary posing-source authorship, current affiliation, verified
Professor/Dr. title (or explicit none), each email's institutional/paper/ORCID
source, problem IDs, and the refusal-table check at the pinned task revision.
Unverified addresses must be omitted with a reason. Check the search explanation
for an explicit empty list; never replace it with guessed recipients. These
proposals do not authorize Agents to edit shared tables. The operator applies
only the exact PASS result and rechecks current refusals and contact history.
For a Mathesis handoff, inspect only the scientific package and allowed public
metadata; no private mail, collaborator promises, credentials or raw logs.
Verify the exact reviewed version of every short note used by the handoff.
Record each task ID and result hash in `note_sources`.
Check the handoff manifest and exact source archive as task artifacts; the
operator's delivery selection must refer to those same preserved bytes. A
verified durable Mathesis identity/source-bound receipt completes Outreach
delivery. Mathesis's later review and public page are downstream responsibilities.

Use the controller's exact review binding and signed verdict contract. PASS
means this version is ready for its next explicitly authorized step. It is not
a publication, sending, authorship or submission decision.

For independent submissions, also read `managed/submission/workflow.json` and
`managed/submission/author-policy.json`. Check actual independence/author facts,
current literature and proof completeness, effective Oracle review and journal
ranking, implemented fixes and venue policy. Inspect the exact extracted source
archive and compiled PDF. A historical ready flag does not replace this review;
an existing version-matched review may be reused. PASS routes the final package
to submission-ready, with separate actual submission authorization and receipt.

Value maximization (the owner 2026-11-01): BOUNCE a note or manuscript whose title
does not name the resolved conjecture or question and its posers, or whose first
abstract sentence does not give its exact posing locator (titles carry no page,
revision, identifier or date locators), that leaves sibling statements of the same source
unmentioned when the same argument settles them, that calls a quickly settled
question open, or that ships internal process wording in the PDF/TeX/archive.

The scientific-core rubric is: fidelity to the posed statement and exact locator;
correctness and completeness of the focused proof; precise distinction between
written, finite and Lean-checked work (including the pinned declaration's scope);
verified references and attribution; and coverage of sibling conjectures, comments
and cross-referenced problems. Packaging wording, layout polish and rebuild
convenience are non-blocking when the scientific content is readable and the
evidence is available for the next authorized step.

## Expert-feedback checklist (2026-11-14)

Distilled from mathematicians' corrections, editor and referee decisions and
recurring review defects. Writers run it before returning a note or manuscript;
reviewers BOUNCE a result that fails an applicable item.

1. Does the title state the mathematics without identifiers, dates, page locators, or internal names?
2. Does the first abstract sentence identify the source question or conjecture, answer, scope, and mechanism?
3. Are prior results, posed questions, and present contributions credited separately?
4. Is every claim labelled proved, cited, computed, formalized, or open?
5. Are all hypotheses, ranges, boundary cases, and finite-prefix limits explicit?
6. Does the introduction explain the closest prior method and the new gain or cost?
7. Is the strongest theorem the paper’s organizing result?
8. Are symbols, domains, maps, normalizations, and implementation-facing objects defined before use?
9. Does the readable proof stand alone, with a pinned Lean link for the formal scope?
10. Are computation ranges, precision, rounding, and certificates stated?
11. Are central quantitative and equality-case steps expanded enough to audit?
12. Does the package contain only cited, reproducible material and a short rebuild path?
13. Does a letter answer the latest question before offering collaboration or context?
14. Does the letter report completed work, explicit open items, and the requested next step?
15. If AI is discussed, is the role, human responsibility, and recipient preference stated honestly?

Layout check for newly authored short notes and new manuscript revisions: check the shared Mathesis LaTeX template pinned in `short_note.manuscript_template` and the guidance in `short_note.layout`. Verify template provenance in the private preparation evidence, readable academic typography, source/PDF consistency and the actual authors' licence. Sections, bibliography style and supporting material follow the mathematics; no fixed page count, section order, coverage table or verification paragraph is required. A necessary package or layout adaptation is allowed when documented. Reuse unchanged approved manuscripts; do not re-typeset a sent or DOI-bearing version for layout alone. Apply the scientific-core rubric above: cosmetic differences alone do not cause a bounce.
